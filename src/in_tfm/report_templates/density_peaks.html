  • Hits are compared by {{ rule.distance }} distance on their Hadamard products.
  • Identical hits are merged into one point with a count. The most common is seen {{ most_copies }} times.
  • Density: 1 / dk, where dk is the distance at which a hit's neighbours, counted with their copies, reach k = {{ rule.k }}.
  • Each hit links to its nearest denser hit (Rodriguez and Laio, 2014). Following the links always ends at a root.
  • A root is at least {{ "%.3f"|format(cuts.delta) }} from anything denser, and at density {{ "%.2f"|format(cuts.rho) }} or more on the decision graph's axis.
  • Both cut-offs are elbows: sort the values largest first, and take the one farthest from the line between the ends.
  • Tree Tn is cluster n in the report.

1. Decision graph

  • Real centres sit top right: dense, and far from anything denser.
  • Top left is outliers: far from everything, but sparse.
  • The offset is half the smallest nonzero dk, so hits at dk = 0 still have a place on the axis.
  • Each axis gets its own elbow, so density and delta are never compared in each other's units.

2. Growth of each tree

  • One row per tree, largest first. Left to right: the root, then hits further from it, in links.
  • Each pair: the mean Hadamard product, then the mean input that produced it. Green is positive, red is negative.
  • Each row has its own colour scale, shared across its stages.
